Closing the Loop: Task-Driven Multimodal Sensing, Perception, and Manipulation

Task-driven sensing, actionable perception, and adaptive physical interaction.

Workshop Scope

Closing the loop means making perception serve action and action improve perception. Task goals guide multimodal sensing. Perception estimates geometry, contact, dynamics, and uncertainty. Manipulation plans and regulates interaction. Each action changes the scene and creates evidence for the next decision.

The workshop connects touch, force, audio, olfaction, vision, and proprioception through their contribution to task-relevant inference and adaptive physical interaction.

The joint proposal brings a complete task-driven loop together with multisensory representations, transfer, and robustness. It gives sensing, perception, and manipulation equal scientific weight.

Why This Topic, Why Now

Open vision-language-action policies such as OpenVLA, dynamic tactile representations such as AnyTouch 2, and adaptive multisensor systems such as MuseVLA make joint design timely. A central challenge is connecting observations and actionable state estimates to real-time planning and control as contacts change and sensor reliability varies. ICRA brings together the hardware, perception, learning, and control communities needed for this discussion.

Scientific Questions and Outcomes

  • How should task goals shape sensing and data acquisition?
  • What must multimodal representations and world models predict for effective planning?
  • How should manipulation use uncertainty, gather evidence, and recover from failure?

The discussion will connect task success, estimation quality, modality contribution, latency, and recovery to practical evaluation guidance and research priorities.

Relevant RAS Technical Committees

  • Robotic Hands, Grasping and Manipulation for physical interaction.
  • Robot Learning for learned representations and policies.
  • Robot Control for feedback and recovery.

Participation and Inclusiveness

The proposed format combines four invited talks, up to five contributed spotlights, an extended poster and demonstration session, and a moderated panel. Talk questions will connect sensing and acquisition, state estimation, multimodal representations, and feedback control. Session chairs will link discussion across talks. Attendees may ask questions in person or submit them in writing before or during the event.

The call will welcome single-modality and component-level work alongside integrated systems. Early-stage, negative-result, reusable-tool, and modest-resource work will be assessed on the support for its claims. Among comparable submissions, early-career authors will receive spotlight priority and facilitated introductions to senior academic and industry participants.

Recruitment through international networks will seek diversity across regions, genders, career stages, and academic and industry perspectives. Industry failure cases will inform discussion, and the invited-speaker list will be assessed against these goals. Presentation guidance will encourage readable slides, defined terminology, and verbal descriptions of visual content. Written questions, seated discussion, and accommodation requests coordinated with ICRA support will provide additional ways to participate.

Publicity and Community Collaboration

Following acceptance, the website will announce speakers, the contribution call, review criteria, publication arrangements, and an accessibility contact. Publicity is planned through institutional networks and robotics, learning, perception, and sensor mailing lists, with targeted outreach to RoboAcoustics and the International Society for Olfaction and Chemical Sensing (ISOCS).

Organizers intend to approach the RAS Technical Committees on Robotic Hands, Grasping and Manipulation, Robot Learning, and Robot Control for call circulation, contributor suggestions, and feedback on the evaluation questions. Committee endorsement is not assumed.

Related Events

This is a new joint workshop, with no previous editions or attendance figures.

  • Act to Sense to Act Better (ICRA 2026, Vienna) addressed multimodal active perception, information-aware action selection, and uncertainty-aware control. This workshop connects those themes to multimodal representations, actionable state estimation, and manipulation and recovery.
  • 7th ViTac Workshop (ICRA 2026, Vienna) connected visual-tactile hardware, simulation, foundation models, and manipulation. This workshop includes additional modalities such as audio and material cues, and links representations to planning, feedback, and recovery.
  • ActiVis (CVPR 2026, Denver) covered perception-action co-design and closed-loop evaluation. This proposal emphasizes heterogeneous physical signals, state estimation, and adaptive manipulation. These are complementary emphases; the comparison does not imply that earlier events excluded these topics.

Materials and Workshop Outcomes

Organizers plan archival proceedings for accepted original papers. The publication route, eligibility of short contributions, copyright and license terms, and camera-ready schedule must be confirmed before the call. Publisher approval and optional indexing are separate; IEEE Xplore inclusion is not assumed.

Within four weeks of the event, Trong Thang Pham plans to publish a checked report after circulating a draft to contributors. Organizers will collect session and panel notes, along with presenter-approved materials. The report will organize accepted work and links to shareable datasets, code, sensor designs, and evaluation tools. It will summarize evaluation guidance on task conditions, sensor availability, estimation quality, task performance, latency, and recovery. It will distill open questions and research priorities from talks, contributed work, and audience questions, recording agreement and disagreement without presenting them as community-wide consensus. It will identify opportunities for shared resources and future collaboration. These outputs do not depend on recording or streaming.
