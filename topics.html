4. Participant Engagement and Inclusiveness

4.1 Audience Interaction and Active Participation

The workshop will combine invited talks, contributed spotlights, an extended poster/demo session, and a moderated panel. Four invited talks will offer complementary perspectives on sensing and acquisition, perception and state estimation, multimodal representations and world models, and manipulation with feedback control. Each talk will include audience Q&A within its allocated time. Session chairs will connect questions across talks, encouraging discussion of how observations become actionable estimates and how physical interaction updates perception. Speakers will be encouraged to identify open problems as well as advances, giving the audience several entry points for discussion.

Up to five contributed spotlights will introduce selected papers and direct attendees to the poster session. The extended poster/demo session, combined with the coffee break, will be the principal setting for detailed technical exchange. Authors can explain methods, hardware, datasets, and results at their posters; demonstrations can show sensing and manipulation capabilities and practical integration issues. Invited speakers and organizers will be encouraged to visit contributed work and introduce researchers with complementary expertise. This format supports questions, feedback, and networking at different technical depths and encourages exchanges across disciplinary backgrounds. Videos and recorded demonstrations will be welcome when transporting hardware is impractical.

The closing panel will bring the four invited speakers together for a 45-minute discussion with substantial audience participation. A moderator will organize questions around the workshop’s scientific themes, including the connection between multimodal representations and control, the role of touch, audio, and olfaction beyond vision and language, robustness to uncertain or missing observations, and the data and evaluation needs of real-world manipulation. The discussion will compare academic and deployment perspectives and invite differing views on the main bottlenecks. Questions can be raised in person or submitted in writing before and during the event; the moderator will balance panel responses with audience contributions.

The final synthesis will highlight recurring questions, lessons from the presentations, and potential directions for collaboration. Organizers will collect discussion notes and presenter-approved materials for the outputs in Section 5. Participants will be invited to suggest resources and open questions for the post-workshop report. These activities connect the program’s scientific contributions to continued community exchange while keeping the half-day format focused and accessible.

4.2 Inclusive Participation Across Communities

The call welcomes single-modality and component-level expertise alongside integrated-system research. Early-stage, negative-result, reusable-tool, and modest-resource work will be assessed on clear, supported claims. Early-career authors receive spotlight priority among comparable submissions and facilitated introductions to senior academic and industry participants.

Recruitment through international networks will seek diversity of regions, genders, career stages, and academic and industry perspectives. Industry failure cases will inform discussion. The confirmed speaker list will be assessed against these goals.

Presentation guidance will cover readable slides, defined terminology, and verbal descriptions of visual content. Written questions, seated discussion, and accommodation requests coordinated with ICRA support access. The call will clearly state the archival policy.

4.3 Publicity and RAS Technical Committee Collaboration

Following acceptance, the website will announce speakers, the call, review criteria, archival publication arrangements, and an accessibility contact. Publicity will use institutional networks and robotics, learning, perception, and sensor mailing lists, with targeted outreach to RoboAcoustics and International Society for Olfaction and Chemical Sensing (ISOCS) groups for audition and chemical sensing.

We intend to approach the RAS TCs on Robotic Hands, Grasping and Manipulation, Robot Learning, and Robot Control for call circulation, relevant contributors, and feedback on the evaluation questions. Endorsement is not assumed. Hanyi Zhang will coordinate outreach; Trong Thang Pham will maintain the website and disseminate outputs under Section 5.

5. Previous Events and Additional Information

5.1 Previous Events or Statement of a New Workshop

This is a new joint workshop, with no previous editions or attendance figures. Its topic overlaps established forums; the focus of this edition is the complete loop connecting physical sensing, state estimation, learned policies, and feedback manipulation.

Act to Sense to Act Better, ICRA 2026, Vienna addressed multimodal active perception, information-aware action selection, and uncertainty-aware control. Our program connects these topics to multimodal representation learning, actionable state estimation, and manipulation/recovery, with contributions and panel discussion spanning the full loop. Event website.

7th ViTac Workshop, ICRA 2026, Vienna connected visual–tactile hardware, simulation, foundation models, and manipulation. This proposal extends the discussion across modalities, including audio and material cues, and emphasizes how perceptual representations support planning, feedback, and recovery across the complete loop. Event website.

ActiVis, CVPR 2026, Denver covered perception–action co-design and closed-loop evaluation. Here, the emphasis is on integrating heterogeneous physical signals with state estimation and adaptive manipulation. These are complementary emphases; we do not claim earlier events excluded them. Event website.
