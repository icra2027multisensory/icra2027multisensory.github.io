3.4 Call for Contributions and Selection Process

We invite 2–4-page original research/work-in-progress papers and 1–2-page demo, dataset, negative-result, or position papers, excluding references. Two nonconflicted reviewers assess task relevance, evidence, clarity, and discussion value. Accepted papers are intended for archival workshop proceedings, with arrangements to be confirmed under Section 5. Up to five receive spotlights. Planned deadlines are eight weeks before the event for submissions, five for decisions, and three for final papers.

5.2 Dissemination of Materials and Workshop Outcomes

We plan archival workshop proceedings for accepted original papers. Before the call is issued, the publication route, eligibility of short contributions, copyright/license, and camera-ready timetable must be confirmed. Submissions must satisfy originality and prior/concurrent publication rules. Publisher approval and optional indexing are separate; IEEE Xplore inclusion is not assumed.

The website will link to archival papers and permitted presentation/demo materials. Trong Thang Pham will collect session and panel notes, circulate the draft to contributors, and publish a checked report within four weeks, including:

Research and resource collection. Accepted papers and approved presentation/demo materials will be organized by their contributions to sensing, perception, and manipulation. Links to shareable datasets, code, sensor designs, and evaluation tools will help participants follow up on work presented at the event.

Evaluation guidance. The report will summarize evaluation issues raised in talks, posters, and the panel, including task conditions, sensor availability, estimation quality, task performance, latency, and recovery. Recommendations will distinguish evidence from individual contributions and broader questions requiring further study.

Research priorities. Open problems and research directions will be distilled from speaker perspectives, contributed work, and audience questions, recording areas of agreement and disagreement. Opportunities for shared resources and future collaboration will be identified without implying a community-wide consensus. Written outputs do not depend on recording or streaming.
